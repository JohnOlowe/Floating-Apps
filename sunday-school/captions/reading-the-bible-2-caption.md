# Caption: Reading the Bible 2 - Hermeneutics and Translations

## Main caption (copy from here)

Every book of the Bible was written with a purpose, an audience, and a literary form. Reading it well means honouring how it was written.

Here are a few takeaways from the session👇

→ Wisdom: Proverbs gives wisdom for living. Ecclesiastes lets you feel the frustration of a broken world. Job shows the world is not ideal, so don't read every verse expecting only promises.

→ Poetry and Songs: Psalms, Lamentations, Song of Solomon are poems, not prose. They are full of metaphor, rhythm, and raw emotion, and they teach us how to pray.

→ Prophecy: The prophets were writing to Israel in their own time, calling them back to faithfulness with warnings of judgement and future hope.

→ Gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke and John give four unique biographical portraits of the same Christ. Pay attention to the parables; earthly stories with a heavenly meaning.

→ Epistles: Read the letters at a go. Follow the logic. Watch for if, then, but, therefore. To understand a part, go back to the previous chapter. Use a study Bible and know the author and audience.

→ Apocalypse: Don't overliteralize Revelation. It was not written to scare you, but to encourage persecuted believers. The picture is simple, God ultimately wins, and creation will be renewed.

→ Translations: Word for word like NASB, KJV, ESV is closest to the original but can be harder to read. Thought for thought like NIV and NLT focuses on readability. Paraphrases drift far and should not be your main study Bible. There is no single best one; study them side by side.

Study it rightly, and let it correct you.

## Shorter version (if you need less text)

How you read the Bible matters as much as that you read it.

Here are a few takeaways from the session👇

→ Wisdom literature is for living. Proverbs to apply, Ecclesiastes and Job to feel the weight of a fallen world.

→ Poetry is poetry, not prose. Psalms teaches us how to pray with raw honesty.

→ Prophecy was first written to Israel in their time, calling them back to God.

→ The Gospels are four testimonies of the same Christ. The Epistles are logical letters; read them at a go and track the therefore.

→ Revelation is not meant to scare you. It was written to encourage persecuted believers that God wins.

Translation matters. Formal like ESV and NASB is closest to the original. Functional like NIV and NLT is easier to read. Use both, but avoid paraphrases as your main Bible.
